Data security and privacy protection have become central topics in modern
computing. Every service that collects user data must balance security
against usability, and privacy protection is no longer optional.

Encryption protects data in transit and at rest, but encryption alone does
not guarantee privacy. Access control, auditing, and data minimization are
equally important for privacy protection. When engineers design a system,
they should ask what data is truly needed, because collecting less data is
the simplest form of privacy protection.

Differential privacy adds calibrated noise to statistics so that individual
records stay private while the aggregate remains useful. Federated learning
keeps raw data on devices and shares only model updates, which reduces the
risk of leaking private data. Secure multiparty computation and homomorphic
encryption allow computation on encrypted data, so privacy is preserved
even during processing.

In this course we study both the theory of data security and the practice
of privacy protection, from access control and encryption protocols to
privacy preserving machine learning.
